# Lighting Rules — OS LESADOS (Classic Daytime-TV Three-Point)

Lighting rules appended to every image prompt.
Goal: warm, welcoming daytime-TV studio look. NEVER noir, NEVER cold cinematic horror.

## Setup (three-point, always)
- **KEY LIGHT**: soft, frontal-to-lateral, slightly above eye level. Defines faces clearly; no harsh nose shadows.
- **FILL LIGHT**: softer, opposite side, lifts shadows. Faces stay readable even in darker set corners (control room, corridors).
- **RIM / BACK LIGHT**: gentle edge separation so characters never melt into the background. Especially important for dark suits and dark hair against dark sets.

## Temperature
- Neutral to slightly warm: **3200K–4500K** feel. Studio warmth, skin tones healthy, set colors vibrant.
- NEVER cold blue, NEVER desaturated teal-and-orange cinema grade, NEVER single-source dungeon lighting.

## Shadows & Contrast
- Shadows soft and diffused (large softbox feel). Moderate contrast only.
- No razor-sharp shadows across faces. No half-face darkness unless the scene action explicitly demands drama — and even then, keep it mild.

## Consistency
- Light direction MUST be identical across all reframes of the same scene (same key side, same shadow fall).
- Control reflections on metal, jewelry, pearls, brooches, lapel pins, monitors: small controlled highlights, no blown-out white blobs.
- Monitors and set screens glow softly; they never overpower the key light on faces.
